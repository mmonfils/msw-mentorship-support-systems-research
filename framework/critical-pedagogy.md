# Critical Pedagogy & Problem-Posing Mentorship

## Deconstructing the "Banking Model" in Field Supervision

Traditional supervisory dynamics often default to a "banking model" of education (Freire, 1970), where knowledge is deposited hierarchically from the field instructor or faculty advisor to the student. This model positions students as passive recipients rather than active agents in their professional formation.

Banking Model:      [ Supervisor ] ---> Deposits Knowledge ---> [ Passive Student ]

Problem-Posing:     [ Mentor & Mentee ] <--- Co-Construct Praxis ---> [ Community Impact ]

## Problem-Posing Praxis in Practice

Critical pedagogy restructures supervision into a collaborative dialogue centered on problem-posing:

1. **Dialogue over Dictation**: Field instruction centers on critical reflection regarding power dynamics within social service agencies.
2. **Co-Construction of Knowledge**: Mentors validate students' lived experiences and community knowledge alongside formal academic literature.
3. **Praxis (Action + Reflection)**: Reflection directly informs student advocacy within their placement agency and broader policy systems.
