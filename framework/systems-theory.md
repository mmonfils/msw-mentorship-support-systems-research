# Systems Theory & Institutional Dynamics

## Person-in-Environment (PIE) in Graduate Education

General Systems Theory provides a lens for evaluating student challenges not as individual deficits, but as interactions across nested environmental systems (Bronfenbrenner, 1979; General Systems Theory).

| Level | System Component | Institutional Friction Point |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Micro** | Student-Supervisor Relationship | Communication breakdown, unclear expectations, lack of psychological safety. |
| **Mezzo** | Field Placement Agency & Academic Unit | Uncompensated internship hours, conflicting agency vs. academic deadlines. |
| **Macro** | Higher Ed Governance & State Licensing | High licensing exam fees, rigid accreditation mandates, policy shifts. |

---

## Macro Analysis via STEEPLE

To understand the broader context affecting MSW students and early-career practitioners, we apply a STEEPLE analysis across seven systemic domains:

* **Social**: Shifting student demographics, increasing numbers of first-generation and working adult learners.
* **Technological**: Reliance on hybrid and online delivery platforms requiring digital access equity.
* **Economic**: Rising tuition costs, uncompensated field practicums, and inflation impacting cost-of-living.
* **Environmental**: Community environmental justice priorities and regional resource distribution.
* **Political**: Legislative changes impacting higher education funding, DEI initiatives, and licensing standards.
* **Legal**: State board licensing requirements, ethical compliance, and liability mandates.
* **Ethical**: Professional duty to prevent burnout and ensure client protection through adequate practitioner support.
